# EV battery technology notes

Lithium-ion battery technology is the dominant energy storage chemistry in
electric vehicles as of 2026. The cathode is typically NMC (nickel manganese
cobalt) or LFP (lithium iron phosphate) depending on the use case.

## Energy density

NMC chemistry pushes ~250 Wh/kg at the cell level; LFP runs lower (~180 Wh/kg)
but with significantly better cycle life and thermal stability. The LFP cells
in consumer EVs are projected to last 6000+ full cycles before reaching 80%
state-of-health, which translates to over 1.5 million miles of driving in a
mid-sized passenger car.

## Thermal management

Liquid cooling is standard for high-performance battery packs. The coolant
loop runs through aluminum cold plates pressed against the cell tabs. Pack
temperature is typically held between 20 and 40 degrees Celsius during
operation; extended exposure above 60 C accelerates degradation by an order
of magnitude.

## Charging

DC fast charging at 350 kW peak is achievable with 800V architectures. Most
production EVs in 2026 use 400V systems with 150-250 kW peak charging. A
typical 10-to-80 percent charge takes 18 to 25 minutes on a fast charger.
